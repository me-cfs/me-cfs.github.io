US Midterms 2026

Amid a historically bleak zeitgeist, the Democratic Party thrashed the Republican Party to see massive electoral gains.

People are struggling and angry, and the incumbent president seemed keen on adding struggles and making people angrier. His electorate noticed and became apathetic. On the other hand, the incumbent’s opponents were fired up to vote and punish his party.

And lo they were punished.

This is not a story of the masses switching from one party to the other. This is a story of one electorate being fired up and the other dejected. Winning Democratic candidates in flipped Senate seats averaged around the same number of votes that Harris did when she lost two years earlier. Republican candidates averaged around two thirds of what Trump received two years earlier.

The Democrats are not popular. Yet they have been given immense institutional power. The hot potato of managing decline has been passed. Unless the unpopular party-elect prepares to deliver popular change, as opposed to managing the historically unpopular status quo, the hot potato will change hands again.

“the hot potato will change hands again.”

This is not a prediction of a 2028 Republican victory. Given US federal governance and voter perceptions of governance are largely based on the president, 2028 is unlikely to be an easy year for Republicans either. A Democratic win in 2028 and a hot potato midterm in 2030 are likely. Although even a massive Republican win in the 2030 midterms may be unlikely to flip the Senate due to the massive advantage Democrats have built this year (Senate elections are staggered, so only around a third of it will be up for re-election in 2030).
